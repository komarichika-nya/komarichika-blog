\subsubsection{dihedral group}
The dihedral group describes the rotation of a regular n-gon in space. Normally, we use generator and relation to describe.
$D_n=\langle r,s\mid r^n=s^2=1,srs=r^{-1}\rangle$. where $r$ is represent for rotation and $s$ is represent for reflect. 
\begin{theorem}
    \[|D_n|=2n\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
    step 1: $|D_n|\le 2n$\\
    According to the relation of $D_n$,$D_n=\langle r,s|r^n=s^2=1,srs=r^{-1}\rangle$
    using $s^2=1\to s=s^{-1}$,using $srs=sr^{-1}\to rs=sr^{-1}$, each word can sweep to left such $r^{i_1}s^{j_1}r^{i_2}s^{j_2}\cdots$,
    until $r^is^j$ or $s^jr^i$, $i\in\{0,1,2,\cdots,n-1\}-\mathrm{Z}^+$ and $j\in\{0,1\}$ so at most have $2n$ elements. $|D_n|\le 2n$\\
    step 2: $|D_n|\ge2n$\\
    Let $R$ is rotation and $S$ is the reflection of ragular n-gon. They satisfy the same relation:
    $R^n=S^2=1,SRS=R^{-1}$
    so there is a homomorphism:
    $\phi:D_n\to\langle R,S\rangle,\phi(r)=R,\phi(s)=S$
    $\langle R,S\rangle$ is generated by $R$ and $S$. The mapping is surjection.$|D_n|\ge|\langle R,S\rangle|\ge 2n$,so $|D_n|=2n$
\end{proof}
\begin{proposition}
    $r^ns=sr^{-n}$. 
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
    $sr^{n}s=r^{-n}\implies sr=r^{-n}s^{-1}\implies sr^n=r^{-n}s$
\end{proof}
There have a beautiful proposition, any rotation can move to the other side of the reflection and the rotation. such $r^isr^j=r^ir^{-j}s=r^{-j}r^{i}s$.\\
Another view is, $D_n$ can describe the orthogonal rouate matrix in 2-d. All of the rouate of $D_n$ consists of the cycle subgroup $C_n$.
Consider a ragular polygon of n-gon, the rouation angle $\theta=\frac{2\pi k}{n}$, so the matrix can be written:
\[R=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta&-\sin\theta\\\sin\theta&\cos\theta\end{pmatrix}\]
where $R^n=I$, $R_0=I$. The reflection can be written:
\[S=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}\]

obviously,$D_{n}\cong\langle R,S\rangle$.

\subsubsection{group action}
$\forall g\in G,\varphi:G\to\mathrm{Sym}(X),\varphi(g)=\varphi_g,\varphi_g:X\to X$.
because $G$ is group, for all $g\in G$, exist inverse. $\varphi_g\circ\varphi_{g^{-1}}=\varphi_{gg^{-1}}=\mathrm{id_X.}$,
$\varphi_{g^{-1}}\varphi_g=\varphi_{g^{-1}g}=\mathrm{id_X.}$, so $\varphi_g$ is a permutation. Generally, both tirvial action($\forall g\in G,\varphi_g=\mathrm{id_X.}$) 
and faithfull action, homomorphic $\varphi$ is not permutation. \\
For $G\curvearrowright X$, from homomorphic mapping, we can see:
\[G \xrightarrow{\hspace{1cm}\iota\hspace{1cm}}
\operatorname{im}\varphi
\xrightarrow{\hspace{1cm}\pi\hspace{1cm}}
\mathrm{Sym}(X)\]
The group action gives us a homomorphic mapping, $\varphi:G\to\operatorname{Sym}(X)$, by the image, we get the subgroup of $\operatorname{Sym}(X)$.
it's also the permutation of $X$, essentally, it's a quotient group $G/\ker\varphi$ in $\operatorname{Sym}(X)$.\\
There is a key point to note here. The kernel of homomorphism and the kernel of group action.
$\ker(G\curvearrowright X)=\{g\in G\mid g\cdot x=x.\forall x\in X\}$, $\ker\varphi=\{g\in G\mid\varphi(x)=\mathrm{id_X.}\}$.
\begin{proposition}
   The kernel of an action of the group G on the set X is the same as the kernel of the corresponding permutation representation.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$g\in\ker(G\curvearrowright X)$,$\forall x\in X$,$gx=x,\varphi(g)(x)=x=\mathrm{id_X.},\varphi(g)=\mathrm{id_X.},\ker(G\curvearrowright X)\subseteq\ker\varphi$.
$\forall g\in G,\varphi(g)=\mathrm{id_X.},gx=\mathrm{id_X.}x=x.$
$g\in\ker(G\curvearrowright X),\ker\varphi\subseteq\ker(G\curvearrowright X)$ 
\end{proof}
