Essentially,rendering is an integral problem.It can be discribed by the following integral form. $$ L_0(x,w_0)=L_e(x,w_0)+\int_{\Omega}f_r(x,w_i,w_0)L_I(x,w_i)(n\cdot w_i)dw $$ where $L_0$ is the light you actually see,$L_e$ is the self-illumination of objects.$f_r$ is bidirectional reflectance distribution function.(BRDF). According to Monte Carlo integral: $$ L_0(x,w_0)=L_e(x,w_0)+\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^N\frac{f_r(x,w_i,w_0)L_I(x,w_i)(n\cdot w_i)}{p(w_i)}\\[10pt]L_0(x,w_0)=L_e(x,w_0)+\frac{f_r(x,w_i,w_0)L_i(x,w_i)(n\cdot w_i)}{p(w_i)} $$ We are concerned with variance. Let $g(w_i)=\frac{f_r(x,w_i,w_0)L_i(x,w_i)(n\cdot w_i)}{p(w_i)}$. Firstly we will prove it is unbiased.Let $I=\int_{\Omega}g(w)dw$, so: $$ I=\int_{\Omega}g(w)dw=\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\frac{g(w_i)}{p(w_i)}\\[10pt]\mathbb{E}[\frac{g(w)}{p(w)}]=\int_{\Omega}\frac{g(w_i)}{p(w_i)}\cdot p(w_i)dw=\int_{\Omega}g(w_i)dw=I $$ According to the defination of variance: $$ \mathbb{Var}[x]=\mathbb{E}[X^2]-\mathbb{E}[X]^2\\[10pt]when:\:n=1\\[10pt]\mathbb{Var_1}=\mathbb{E}[(\frac{g(w)}{p(w)})^2]-\mathbb{E}[\frac{g(w)}{p(w)}]^2\\[10pt]=\int_{\Omega}\frac{f^2(w)}{p(w)}dw-I^2\\[10pt]\\\mathbb{Var}[I]=\frac{1}{N}(\int_{\Omega}\frac{f^2(w)}{p(w)}dw-I^2) $$ We want to the variance as small as possible.$\min(\int_{\Omega}\frac{f^2(w)}{p(w)}dw)\land\int_{\Omega}p(w)dw=1$ To solve this,I will offer two methods.Firstly,This is a optimization problem. We can construct the Lagrange multipliers. $$ \mathcal{L}(x,y,\lambda)=f(x,y)-\lambda(g(x,y)-1)\\[10pt]=f(x,y)-\lambda g(x,y)+\lambda\\[10pt]=\int_{\Omega}\frac{f^2(w)}{p(w)}dw-\lambda \int_{\Omega}p(w)dw+\lambda\\[10pt]\\ $$ We review this function.$\forall p$ equal to $\int p=1$,which one makes minmizes $\int\frac{f^2}{p}$ ? We can use $$ let:p\to p+c*h(w)\:when\:c\to0\\[10pt]let:\frac{d}{dc}\int_{\Omega}(\frac{f^2(w)}{p(w)+c*h(w)}-\lambda(p(w)+c*h(w)))=0\\[10pt]\int_{\Omega}\frac{\partial}{\partial c}(\frac{f^2(w)}{p(w)+c*h(w)}-\lambda(p(w)+c*h(w)))=0\\[10pt]\int_{\Omega}-\frac{f^2(w)}{(p(w)+c*h(w))^2}*h(w)-\lambda*h(w)=0\\[10pt]\int_{\Omega}(-\frac{f^2(w)}{p^2(w)}-\lambda)*h(w)=0\\[10pt]-\frac{f^2(w)}{p^2(w)}-\lambda=0\\[10pt]p(w)=\frac{|f(w)|}{\sqrt{\lambda}} $$ So we get: $$ \int_{\Omega}\frac{f(w)}{\sqrt{\lambda}}=1\\[10pt]\int_{\Omega}f(w)=\sqrt{\lambda}\\[10pt]p(w)=\frac{|f(w)|}{\int_{\Omega}f(w)} $$ Another way we can use Cauchy-Schwarz: $$ (\int_{\Omega}\frac{f^2(w)}{p(w)}dw)(\int_{\Omega}p(w)dw)>=\int_{\Omega}\sqrt{\frac{f^2(w)}{p(w)}\cdot p(w)}dw\\[10pt]\int_{\Omega}\frac{f^2(w)}{p(w)}>=\int_{\Omega}|f(w)|\\[10pt] $$ if and only if $\frac{\frac{f^2(w)}{p(w)}}{|f(w)|}=contanst$ ,The equality holds. so: $$ p=\frac{f}{contanst},contanst=\int_{\Omega}f(w)dw $$ We have discoverd a paradox.We want to find the integral appers in the expression for the optimal solution.This cause we can't find the optimal solution,only approximate.In next section,I will introduce some optimization methods.

$$ L_0(x,w_0)=L_e(x,w_0)+\int_{\Omega}f_r(x,w_i,w_0)L_i(x,w_i)(n,w_i)dw_i\\[10pt]L_0(x,w_0)=L_e(x,w_0)+\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\frac{f_r(x,w_i,w_0)L_I(x,w_i)(n*w_i)}{p(w_i)}\\[10pt]let:n=1\\[10pt]L_0(x,w_0)=L_e(x,w_0)+\frac{f_r(x,w,w)L_I(x,w)(n*w)}{p(w)}\\[10pt]f_r(x,w_i,w_0)=\frac{dL_r(w_0)}{dE_i(w_i)}=\frac{dL_0(w_0)}{dL_i\cos \theta}\cdot \frac{1}{sr}\\[10pt]define:M\:is\:irradance\\[10pt]M=\rho\cdot E_i\\[10pt]\int_{\Omega}L_0\cos\theta dw_0=L_0\cdot \pi=\rho\cdot E\:(Lambertian\:Material)\\[10pt]f_r(w_i\to w_0)=\frac{\rho}{\pi}\\[10pt]L_0(x,w_0)=L_e(x,w_0)+\frac{\frac{\rho}{\pi}\cdot L_I(x,w)(n*w)}{p(w)}\\[10pt]if:is\:object\to L_e(x,w_0)=0\\[10pt] $$

Before introduce solution2,we will introduce the concept of soild angle. Soild angle is used to measure the range of a given point from a given filed of view.It be defined in $\Omega=\frac{A}{r^2}$,where $A$ is the area of the hemisphere and $r$ is the radius of the sphere.Particularly,the whole sphere is $4\pi$， Given an any curved surface $S$,what is its the soild angle ? $$ \Omega=\int\int_{S}\frac{\hat r\cdot\hat n}{r^2}dS $$ where $\hat n$ is the normal vector of a small area $dS$. Image you are stand on the center of sphere,you want to what is the actual area I see when I look at the center of this sphere?That must be the projection of the plane normal onto the line of sight.Then divide the actual observed by $r^2$ to obtain the solid angle. In physics,the spherical cooridinate system is defined by $(r,\theta,\varphi)$,$\theta$ is azimuth and $\varphi$ is polar angle.When the $\theta$ change,The arc length that varies along the meridian is $rd\theta$ ,The arc length that varies along the parallel of latitude is $r\sin\theta d\theta d\varphi$,so the $dA$ is: $$ dA=rd\theta* rd\sin\theta d\theta=r^2\sin\theta d\theta d\varphi $$ so $d\Omega$ is : $$ d\Omega=\sin\theta d\theta d\varphi $$ Another brilliant way ti understand it is caculate the ratio and then remap. $$ let:dS'=dS(\hat n\cdot\hat r)\\ \frac{dS'}{A}=\frac{dS(\hat n\cdot\hat r)}{4\pi r^2}\\d\Omega=4\pi*\frac{dS(\hat n\cdot\hat r)}{4\pi r^2}=\frac{dS(\hat n\cdot\hat r)}{r^2} $$ sol2: This method's core idea is we sampled the light source. $$ define :light\to S\\[10pt]\forall x\in S,p(x)=\frac{1}{S}\\[10pt]d\omega=\frac{dA\cos\theta}{r^2}\\[10pt]p(\omega)=\frac{dA}{Ad\omega}=\frac{\cos\theta}{Ar^2}\\[10pt]\\[10pt]so:L_0=L_I*\frac{\rho}{\pi}*\frac{\cos\theta\cos\theta'}{r^2}*A\\[10pt]let:G(x,x')=\frac{\cos\theta\cos\theta'}{r^2} $$ where $p(x)$ is the probability and $G(x,x')$ is discrible the visibility of two soild angle. core code:
